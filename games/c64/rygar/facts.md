# Rygar facts

All addresses refer to the expanded C64 program in the Softrambo and Wanderer Group image. The listing uses the handover at `$0915`. The first-round snapshot is the comparison image.

## Memory and video

| Address | What it holds or does |
| --- | --- |
| `$09DB`, `$09DF` | Selects CPU port value `$35`, leaving the hardware I/O visible with BASIC and KERNAL replaced by RAM, and VIC bank `$4000`. |
| `$0E3D`, `$0E84` | Installs and handles the hardware raster IRQ. The handler dispatches on the raster position, without chaining through the KERNAL IRQ vector. |
| `$4400` | The 40-column screen matrix. `$0A68` copies three upper and three lower rows from the templates at `$9000` and `$90F0`, with matching colour tables at `$9078` and `$9168`. |
| `$4300`, `$47F8` | Software sprite records are eight bytes apiece. `$1FA8` uploads slots 8–15 to the hardware registers and writes their eight shape pointers. The lower-panel phase at `$25AA` reuses five sprites for power icons. |
| `$4CC0`–`$6FFF` | 141 64-byte shape slots from pointers `$33` through `$BF`. The first 63 bytes of each hold 21 rows of packed multicolour pixels. `$2482` produces mirrored copies using the table generated by `$2436`. |
| `$7000`, `$7800` | The playfield reads its glyphs at `$7000`, rebuilt from a terrain bank by `$11AA` or from the panel glyphs by `$17C6`. The lower panel selects `$7800`. |
| `$E000`–`$FFFF` | Four terrain glyph banks in RAM under the KERNAL. `$11AA` copies `$800` bytes from the selected bank. The active hardware vectors occupy the final six RAM bytes after initialisation. |
| `$9800`–`$9BFF` | Four 256-byte terrain colour pages. `$119E` selects one using the same bank index as the character set. |
| `$9C00`–`$AFFF` | Sixteen round terrain records, each `$140` bytes. `$11D5` computes the round base and reads the first 40 tile IDs. Edge drawing reads groups of four tile IDs as the camera moves. |
| `$B000`–`$BFFF` | 256 metatile definitions of 16 screen codes, arranged as four rows of four. `$1246` multiplies a tile ID by 16. |
| `$3000`–`$34FF` | Sixteen 80-byte round event records. `$0ACA` copies one through the pointer table at `$0C7D` to the working buffer at `$2B8B`. `$26EC` and `$26F0` examine the edge column's events. |
| `$3500`–`$357F` | Sixteen eight-byte object queue seeds copied backwards into `$43E0` by the round setup. |
| `$8000`–`$8E77` | Three-voice music driver, cue directories, event streams and sound-effect presets. Cue selection is through `$8000` and `$8AA7`. Effect selection is through `$86D0` and `$8759`. |

The static panel font has blank at `$00`, A–Z at `$54`–`$6D` and 0–9 at `$6E`–`$77`. `$1DC5` translates ASCII-letter and digit bytes by arithmetic and maps period to `$79`. The title's eight lines start at `$1CFB`. The prologue at `$1E7A` and ending at `$1F5B` use high-bit line separators and a final `$FE`. The panel's power-slot frame glyphs occupy `$AF`–`$B7`.

## Controls and update order

- `$1089` samples port 2 through `$DC00` and combines it with five keys. Bits 0 through 4 of `$6B` are active-low up, down, left, right and fire. The title identifies Shift, Space, Z, X and Return as the keyboard equivalents.
- `$1262` uses the input and the cells adjacent to Rygar to choose the scroll direction. Positive scroll requests at `$12F5` shift the landscape right and negative requests shift it left. Four two-pixel changes make a character-column shift.
- `$22CA` and `$2093` handle ascent and the weapon separately. With fire released, up can start the jump at `$2303`. Fire can start the raised attack at `$20B1` when up is held. Down can enter the crouch pose.
- `$28BA` starts the weapon and body collision checks. The game uses VIC sprite collision bits saved at `$2BF1`, then compares the candidate coordinates and status. `$29B5` awards points and starts the hit animation. `$2A5E` distinguishes pickup types and can set flags for powers, a life or extra time.
- `$252B` changes Rygar's pose to 4 and decrements lives when the fall or hazard condition is met. `$21F7` runs the death animation and resets the current powers and timer before a retry.
- `$1717` manages the round boundary. `$1733` increments the zero-based round index `$1E`. `$1737` compares against 16, with the terminal path calling the ending text routine at `$1E25`.
- `$2D67` adds to a 24-bit score or bonus field. `$2DB9` converts it to decimal digits and `$2DE9` draws those digits. `$18BB` tallies the round bonuses. `$1B74` takes initials and `$2CE3` reorders the score records.
- `$0D75`, `$0DB5` and `$0DEF` scan F1, F3 and F5 in the keyboard matrix. Their paths suspend the timer, enter the game-over routine, and cycle a four-state sound control respectively.
- `$8136` advances music and effects. The music renderer writes frequency, pulse, envelope and control registers for three voices. Effects use voices 1 and 3. `$86D0` patches both source bytes of its indexed copy from the selected 20-byte effect record before reading it.

## Live checks

- The Round 1 raster capture recorded stored `$D018` values `$1C` at line 82 and `$1E` at line 211, with CIA2 port A at `$92`. In a separate 50-frame capture from the neutral play snapshot there were 200 hits at `$0E84`, 50 each at `$0F14`, `$0F35`, `$1089`, `$0FA7` and `$8136`, and 100 at the fractional timer routine `$0FD2`. The renderer scheduled four interrupts per PAL frame in this state.
- A breakpoint at `$1098` and two instruction steps with right and fire held produced `A=$E7` after the CIA read and `$6B=$E7` after the store. Stops on `$111D` followed by a step produced `$FB` for Z, `$F7` for X, `$EF` for Return, `$FD` for Space and `$FE` for matrix row 6, column 4.
- With up alone, six frames reached the jump start at `$2303` once and the attack start zero times. With up and fire, the same snapshot reached the attack start at `$20B1` once and the jump start zero times. The IRQ control breakpoint counted 24 passes in each case.
- After setting the hazard flag at `$3B` for an isolated death-handler test, the store watch on `$65` stopped at `$2551`, after the decrement at `$254F`. Lives were four and `$4375` was 4. This checks the response to the flag.
- Load watches during a controlled 8-frame right-and-fire test counted eight CPU reads in Round 1's map and 16 in the metatile definitions. The secondary layout at `$47E8`–`$4CBF` had no CPU read in that window. Its first three fields largely correspond to the active map at an offset of `$5800`. The alternate Triton release with both trainers off retains this same block apart from four sprite pointer cells. Its meaning as a second layout is supported by the record structure and matching data. A reader for it outside the observed window is open.
- The same alternate handover matches every byte of the sprite bank, the template/map area at `$9800`–`$BFFF`, the tail at `$7E00`–`$7FFF` and the secondary metatile tail at `$8E78`–`$8FFF`. The six bytes at `$FFFA`–`$FFFF` differ. The selected image arrives with RAM vector values already in the last three words. In the alternate handover they complete the last glyph. The game writes the vectors at `$0E44`–`$0E59` before the new round plays.
- The analysed disk has one PRG and no independent overlays. The 50-frame trace had no execute hit at the KERNAL LOAD entry `$FFD5`, while its IRQ control counted 200. The terrain directory and its address arithmetic account for all sixteen round layouts in the one image.

## Open interpretation

The purpose of the retained border row at `$1892`, the small numeric runs at `$2F33`, and any reader of the secondary map beyond the measured window are open. They are included in the listing as loaded data. The appearance of the final round and the full high-score flow are traced in code.
