# Impossible Mission II analysis history

The installed VICE-MCP headless executable and its dummy audio wrapper were kept. The Sharks D64 reached the intro but the custom serial reader stayed around $BE40–$BE6E polling CIA2. The Nostalgia D64 reached NOSDOS and stayed in its serial loading path. The publisher G64 passed control to its boot code at $02E0 but did not reach a playable display in this setup. The tape release worked after pressing Play when the bootstrap was waiting for pulses.

The kit's TAP reader provisionally divided stretches of this recording into generic turbo pages. The decoded pages did not match the RAM at the reported addresses. Reading the pulse reader and testing the two measured pulse widths against the snapshots recovered the actual named format. The private decoder, alignment diagnostics and original generic extract are in `work/`.

A trial seek after restoring a snapshot without an attached TAP crashed VICE. Reattaching the tape before seeking restored reliable positioning. The final sequence and scoreboard were then reached by setting the program counter to their original resident load paths. A forced record 315 probe at offset 649298 skipped the header while the monitor resumed the live machine. Moving the seek back to 646000 and disabling warp until the probe was armed caught the copy and matched all 3,120 bytes.

The disassembler initially walked code beyond the logged addresses and created false entries in the boot screen. That region was reset to undefined after matching it to the BASIC startup screen. The high-RAM character sets were identified by live writes to the VIC memory selector. The resident listing is built from the $0880 hand-over because it preserves startup code overwritten by subsequent screen setup.

Retyping the resident compiler's inline frames and jump tables removed false operands emitted by linear decoding. Reimporting that map also dropped an automatic span at $03D7–$03DE. Those eight bytes match the title's bootstrap cassette fragment at the same addresses and lie outside the recorded resident load ranges.

The resumed title analysis traced the voice gate operand at $14DA and classified the NMI paths that select their next entry through the low vector byte. Restarting Regenerator on the exported title snapshot minted automatic aliases inside packed streams and bitmap data. Its raw export dropped the earlier coverage by splitting described spans. The rebuilt title symbol map retains the established block and symbol boundaries and takes the deliberate annotations from the live export. The local project was regenerated from that map.

The next resident pass recovered the room renderer's patched calls and the actor script's indirect dispatch from their pointer tables. Targeted VICE probes checked the patched JSR operand, three script entries, an elevator rectangle read, a raster sprite position write and two room workspace writes. Recursive disassembly from the sprite IRQ also reached the inline frames and target tables as if they were code. Reapplying the established types removed the false references inside the agent sprite bank.

The next step 50 pass annotated the agent sprite update, object-search countdown, room drawing and melody-selection routines. VICE write watchpoints and instruction checkpoints confirmed the sprite carry and delta handling and the conditions for decrementing the object countdown. The local probe scripts, outputs and annotation batches are in ignored work/. The exported resident symbols and four rebuilt listings account for 77,313 of 91,814 tracked bytes across the game.

The next resident pass followed the room transition handlers, search display cache, actor update and sprite-band dispatch, and the panel counter paths. VICE hit the four recovered indirect targets and bounded the actor contact test. A last-byte store watch justified removing the 160-byte actor workspace from the denominator. A separate watch during the record 315 copy showed that the high RAM previously typed as instructions contains copied data and later runtime tables. The resident export and all four rebuilt listings record the pass. The aggregate ledger accounts for 82,265 of 91,654 tracked bytes. Step 50 remains open.

The resumed pass stopped record 315 at the final page-copy store and proved that it delivers agent sprite images and a fixed-address speech driver. It added the scene resource part from that loader state, preserving the 48-byte suffix at its original staging address. Instruction and store checkpoints verified the resident calls into the speech driver, the first SID volume write, the three sprite pointers and the two alternating sprite-band writes. The panel rectangle probes checked both source glyph reads and their corresponding destination writes. A separate stop at $ACEA showed that the record 267 copy covers $C000 through $E3FF and that $E400 through $E48F still contains title bitmap bytes. The five rebuilt listings have zero uncounted stretches, with 91,930 of 94,528 tracked bytes explained.

The follow-up pass traced the four raster sprite-band entries, reproduced three title instrument copies using store watches, and watched the three coordinate-table reads at $40A3. The scene resource's $FB06–$FB3F gap contains an x86 instruction fragment between the speech code and the sprite data. The resident and scene maps were re-exported and all five listings rebuilt. The ledger counts 93,496 of 94,528 bytes explained, and each rebuilt listing has zero uncounted stretches. The resident and scene queues remain open at step 50.
